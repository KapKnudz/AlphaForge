# Contrasts from valuation reviews

These qualitative cases distill real review failures without issuer identities,
figures or private receipts. Each conclusion is conditional on the stated facts,
not a permanent classification. Public principles are linked by identifier in
[principles](principles.md); software admission lives in [owners](alphaforge.md).

## 1. Returns: a familiar percentage is not a matched operand

**Temptation:** A retailer discloses strong ROCE; copy it into operating ROIC and
then call it the future incremental return funding growth.

**Why it fails:** Its ROCE uses EBIT plus financial income over monthly-average
capital employed. After-tax operating profit over reconciled operating capital
has a different numerator, capital definition and timing. Even a correctly
measured historical operating return describes existing investments, not an
observed future marginal return. [P1]

**Distinguishing evidence:** Read the ratio definition and capital reconciliation;
match tax, leases, cash, goodwill, period and currency to earnings. Ask whether new
projects have comparable economics and whether capacity/replacement is funded.
A bank-like financing business would also disconfirm the operating-company basis.

**Justified conclusion:** The disclosed ROCE can describe the issuer's own metric,
but cannot substitute for those two other concepts. Ask: “Which reviewed operating
capital/earnings reconciliation and explicit future-return premise support this
forecast?”

**Valid contrast:** A reviewed operating-business history matches earnings and
capital on all relevant bases, and an analyst explicitly supports using that
historical ratio as the initial forecast return. That is a usable *assumption*,
subject to the method's admission rules, not proof about future projects.

## 2. Earnings: neither reported nor adjusted means recurring

**Temptation:** A profitable supplier's reported EBIT includes a gain from reducing
an acquisition earn-out liability. Treat total operating income as customer sales
and project the gain indefinitely. Alternatively, replace reported EBIT with the
larger management-adjusted EBIT simply because it is labeled adjusted.

**Why it fails:** Liability remeasurement is not customer sales. Reported results
and continuing earnings answer different questions; a reconciliation is required.
Costs called exceptional may recur, and higher adjusted earnings need not be more
representative. [P2]

**Distinguishing evidence:** Reconcile sales, other income, reported/adjusted EBIT,
tax effects, settlement mechanics and multi-year recurrence. If EUR earnings are
combined with SEK capital, a seemingly plausible return remains unmatched until
an explicit compatible FX basis is established. Check whether a net-debt bridge
already deducts the cash/non-operating asset before adding it again. [P1, P2]

**Justified conclusion:** Preserve reported facts and identify the recurring-basis
question. A source-backed normalization may be economically appropriate, but it
is not permission to overwrite an implementation's reported-input contract.

**Valid contrast:** Sources reconcile a genuinely isolated gain and support its
exclusion from a continuing forecast, with consistent taxes and capital treatment.
Conversely, repeated restructuring costs belong in a recurring cost allowance,
not an annual “one-off” add-back. Ordinary recurring sales require no exclusion
just because this teaching case had other income.

## 3. Acquired capital: closing ownership is not full-period earnings

**Temptation:** A technology licensor closes an acquisition near year end. Average
opening and closing capital and divide the year's profit by it as though the
acquired business contributed earnings for that whole year.

**Why it fails:** The closing balance contains acquired assets/goodwill and
consideration obligations, while the earnings period has little or no acquired
profit. An average does not repair that perimeter mismatch. Acquisition-date
measurement and disclosure do not turn closing assets into full-year operations.
[P1, P3]

**Distinguishing evidence:** Acquisition/control date, actual consolidation dates,
profit contribution, purchase-price allocation, contingent consideration and
lease basis. Check any pro-forma full-year earnings separately: estimates are not
reported results. Removing all goodwill or ignoring consideration is not a
self-evident cure.

**Justified conclusion:** Refuse an unqualified matched-return inference, not all
valuation of acquisitive firms. Ask for a reviewed consistent perimeter and
capital/earnings timing basis; if the implementation cannot represent that basis,
report that separate limitation.

**Valid contrast:** A business held throughout the earnings period has compatible
opening/closing capital and full-period earnings, with reconciled acquisition
and lease accounting. Acquisition history alone does not disqualify it. A future
combined-business forecast may also be defensible with explicit integration and
funding premises; it is not automatically an admitted mature profile.

## 4. Evidence: complete documents do not qualify a valuation

**Temptation:** Every expected annual/quarterly PDF is retained and a packet
validates, so declare the issuer valuation-ready. Or read an undated-ROIC refusal
as a request to attach today's date to a provider scalar.

**Why it fails:** Document coverage, usable financial history, source-qualified
capital, method suitability and actual analyst review are separate propositions.
A packet hash binds the stable packet identity projection, excluding run
timestamps retained in stored JSON; it does not authenticate accounting
interpretations or make old classifications current. Publication, observation
and ingestion dates are different facts. [AlphaForge owners](alphaforge.md)

**Distinguishing evidence:** Current packet/source anchors, selected fiscal history,
original currency, capital/earnings reconciliation and genuinely dated review.
A metadata limitation or a missing later prerequisite can survive full coverage.
A path error loading authentic bytes is an IO problem, not financial disproof.

**Justified conclusion:** State what is complete and what remains unqualified.
Read the detailed refusal operands and layer; do not manufacture dates or infer
approval from a compatibility reason string.

**Valid contrast:** Complete current evidence plus compatible numerical history,
reviewed premises and an actually admitted method can support proceeding. An
incomplete unrelated document inventory need not defeat a separate valuation
method whose own material evidence is complete—unless the applicable contract
requires that inventory. Read the owner rather than invent an exception.

## 5. Tests and refusals: a solver result has a scope

**Temptation:** Many tests pass and a forward valuation succeeds, so claim that a
public reverse solver works throughout its declared search interval. A negative
growth sample refuses; skip it or shrink the bracket until a root appears.

**Why it fails:** Tests prove their exercised assertions, not economic admission
for every candidate. Declining capital, new financing or changing margins may
cross a model boundary. Altering the interval changes the question; one bounded
root cannot certify the advertised full range or establish uniqueness.
[AlphaForge owners](alphaforge.md)

**Distinguishing evidence:** Trace the public caller, exact fixed assumptions,
eligibility, declared bounds and first refusing candidate through the same
forward evaluator. Distinguish invalid economics, insufficient evidence,
no crossing, nonconvergence and conditional candidate solutions. A different
target price does not cure an unsupported economic candidate.

**Justified conclusion:** Retain the refusal and flag any capability/contract
mismatch. A qualified forward result may survive an unavailable reverse axis.
Do not claim current reverse coverage from a proposed specification.

**Valid contrast:** An explicitly admitted range whose sampled candidates are
financially valid can yield a conditional root or an honest no-crossing outcome.
Neither means the growth assumption is uniquely identified or endorsed. Tests
through the actual public path improve software evidence; independent source and
financial checks are still required.

## 6. Method suitability: refusal is not universal impossibility

**Temptation:** Apply industrial FCFF/operating ROIC to a deposit-taking bank
because its EBITDA is positive. Or treat positive EBITDA and a profitable quarter
as sufficient for a mature positive-NOPAT method despite full-year operating losses.

**Why it fails:** Bank funding and regulatory capital do not fit the ordinary
industrial debt/reinvestment interpretation. EBITDA excludes depreciation; one
quarter does not establish annual continuing profitability. A narrow mature-lane
refusal does not prove the business is worthless or that every DCF is impossible.
[P4; P2 for the earnings basis]

**Distinguishing evidence:** Actual business activities, regulatory capital,
full-period EBIT, cash needs, cycle/transition, segment mix and method inputs.
A commercial software company serving banks is not itself a bank. The customer
sector does not determine the supplier's economics.

**Justified conclusion:** For a bank, consider an equity-based method with credible
capital/distribution assumptions; for a loss-stage operator, ask for an evidenced
transition and financing basis before proposing a suitable model. In AlphaForge,
conceptual alternatives remain proposals unless the contract supports them.

**Valid contrast:** A nonfinancial supplier with compatible positive operating
history, reviewed stable margins and funded capacity can suit operating FCFF.
A bank with reliable book equity, expected ROE, capital requirements and payout
premises can suit equity valuation; that does not authorize AlphaForge to run an
unimplemented method. Refuse only the unsupported premise or implementation.
