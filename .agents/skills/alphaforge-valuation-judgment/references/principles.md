# Public financial principles

These are financial reasoning sources, not AlphaForge policy. URLs were checked
on 2026-10-07. Page references below are physical PDF pages. Source material can
be historical: apply current accounting standards and the actual issuer's
reviewed disclosures rather than importing old accounting treatments wholesale.

## P1 — Return basis and forecast return

Aswath Damodaran, NYU Stern, *Return on Capital (ROC), Return on Invested Capital
(ROIC) and Return on Equity (ROE): Measurement and Implications* (July 2007):
[paper](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/papers/returnmeasures.pdf),
pp. 4–6 (existing assets versus future investments), 7–11 (after-tax operating
income, invested capital, cash and timing), 48–52 (forecasting and average versus
marginal returns).

A return measure is meaningful only with matched earnings and capital. Historical
operating returns are evidence about assets in place, not measurements of future
project returns. The paper discusses beginning-period capital and averaging as
alternative timing conventions; AlphaForge's particular averaging requirement
belongs to its own contract, not a universal definition of ROIC. Similarly,
ROCE labels are not standardized evidence that the numerator is after-tax or the
capital definition is operating-only: read the issuer definition.

## P2 — Continuing earnings and double counting

Damodaran, *Investment Valuation*, chapter 9, *Measuring Earnings*:
[chapter](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/valn2ed/ch9.pdf),
pp. 21–23 (one-time versus recurring/unusual items), pp. 23–25 (investment income
and cross-holdings).

A forecast base should reflect continuing operations. A nonannual expense can
still recur; a management “adjusted” label is not sufficient evidence to remove
it. Separate non-operating income from operating earnings and treat the
corresponding asset consistently in the value bridge to avoid double counting.
This source's historical goodwill-amortization discussion is not a current IFRS
accounting instruction. Earnings normalization is an explicit judgment requiring
source reconciliation, not a mechanical upward adjustment.

## P3 — Acquisition measurement and period mismatch

IFRS Foundation, [IFRS 3 Business Combinations](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ifrs-3-business-combinations/),
“About” summary: acquisition consideration and identifiable assets/liabilities are
measured at fair value, residual cost allocated to goodwill, and disclosures help
users evaluate the acquisition's nature and financial effects.

That authoritative summary supports inspecting acquisition accounting; it does
not prescribe a particular ROIC averaging convention. The period/perimeter
lesson is a reasoning consequence of P1: capital newly present at closing cannot
be assumed to have generated all the preceding year's profit. Verify actual
control/consolidation dates and earnings contribution in the issuer disclosures.
Contingent consideration, goodwill and leases require coherent treatment across
capital, earnings and the equity bridge; no blanket exclusion rule follows.

## P4 — Financial firms need a different valuation basis

Damodaran, *Valuing Financial Service Firms* (April 2009):
[paper](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/papers/finfirm09.pdf),
pp. 7–10 (debt, regulatory constraints and difficulty estimating reinvestment),
and its equity-valuation discussion.

Bank debt/deposits serve the business rather than fitting the usual industrial
financing split. Equity-based valuation can be more meaningful, but requires
credible capital and distribution premises. This is method suitability, not an
endorsement of any particular bank or permission to use an unsupported software
route. Losses likewise call for explicit future economics, not the conclusion
that valuation is universally impossible.

Document completeness, test coverage and software refusal semantics are
engineering/evidence claims: their authority is the repository contract and
observed execution, not these financial papers.
